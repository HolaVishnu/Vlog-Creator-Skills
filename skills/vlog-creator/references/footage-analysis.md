# Footage Analysis

Read this when inventorying source media, choosing usable takes, detecting bad or duplicate clips, building an edit decision list, or finding missing coverage.

Assign every source a stable ID such as `A001`, `A002`, and `A003`; keep that ID unchanged across inventories, notes, manifests, timelines, caption maps, and fallback packages. Record technical metadata beside semantic notes: path, duration, resolution, frame rate, audio presence, orientation, who or what appears, strongest line or image, emotional tone, continuity details, and useful source time ranges.

## Clip Categories

| Category | Mark when it contains | Editing use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KEEP | Clear story beat, reaction, useful dialogue, reveal, or payoff | Primary timeline |
| CUT | Repetition, accidental capture, confusing detour, unusable media, or no story job | Exclude unless context changes |
| B-ROLL | Place, action, detail, movement, or texture | Cover cuts, establish setting, show proof |
| AUDIO | Clean narration, dialogue, ambience, room tone, or natural sound | Voice track, ambience bed, SFX source |
| STILL | Image, screenshot, map, receipt, graphic, or frame grab | Visual cover, title, context, end card |
| TRANSITION | Natural movement, door, whip, match action, location change | Bridge scenes sparingly |
| GRAPHIC | Fact, name, location, price, route, step, or comparison needs text | Caption, lower third, label, title |
| COLOR | Exposure, white balance, flicker, or match issue | Correction and continuity note |
| STORY | Question, turn, answer, conflict, emotion, or conclusion | Structure and retention |

## Highlights And Bad Clips

Score each potential select for story value, clarity, emotion, uniqueness, technical quality, and repairability. A flawed but irreplaceable answer can remain KEEP with a repair note; a beautiful but repetitive shot becomes B-ROLL or CUT.

Flag bad clips separately from creative decisions:

- Corrupt or unsupported media.
- Missing or unreadable audio/video streams.
- Clipped dialogue, wind, hum, echo, or abrupt level changes.
- Severe shake, focus miss, motion blur, flicker, rolling shutter, or exposure shifts.
- Duplicates, near-duplicates, accidental captures, or long dead sections.
- Mixed frame rates, mixed orientation, low resolution, or codec problems.

Detect duplicates by comparing filename patterns, duration, creation times, technical metadata, perceptual similarity when tools support it, and content notes. Keep the clearest or most emotionally useful version; mark the others CUT or backup B-ROLL with the reason.

## Coverage Gaps

Compare the intended story against the available shots. Can the viewer identify the setting, understand the goal, see the process, feel the turn, and understand the payoff? If not, propose the safest workaround: use available B-roll, narration, title text, a still image, a pickup shot, or an honest omission.
