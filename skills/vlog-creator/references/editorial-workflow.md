# Editorial Workflow

Read this when deciding the vlog's story, choosing clip order, setting pace, planning transitions, or running a retention pass.

Start with a one-sentence viewer promise: what will the viewer experience, learn, or feel if they stay? The opening should show a result, question, surprise, risk, emotional turn, or visually specific destination. Avoid a generic greeting before anything interesting happens unless the creator's personality is already the hook.

## Story Shapes

| Shape | Use when | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journey | Travel, events, day-in-life, process | Hook, departure/context, discoveries, turn, payoff |
| Problem to solution | Fix, challenge, transformation | Problem, attempts, obstacle, breakthrough, result |
| Challenge checkpoints | Timed goals, budget, fitness, creator tasks | Rules, checkpoints, setbacks, final score |
| Before and after | Makeover, learning, lifestyle change | Before, process, reveal, reflection |
| Reflective diary | Personal or emotional vlog | Present moment, memory/context, realization, close |
| Faceless explainer | Voiceover-led or visual essay | Claim, evidence, examples, conclusion |

Choose the structure that the footage can honestly support. Do not force a trendy format onto quiet material.

## Selection And Pacing

Keep clips that change information, feeling, or energy. Remove repeated setup, dead air that has no emotional purpose, confusing detours, and duplicate angles that say the same thing. Preserve natural pauses when they carry humor, disbelief, warmth, or credibility.

Use pacing contrast:

- Fast: travel logistics, repeated actions, setup, montages, obvious transitions.
- Medium: explanations, arrival, demonstrations, setup for payoffs.
- Slow: reactions, reveals, important emotional beats, atmosphere.

Transitions should be motivated by action, location, time, thought, or music. Avoid effects that merely announce the edit.

For long-form event, concert, wedding, ceremony, or performance edits, do not treat length as the enemy. If the viewer promise is "enjoy the full songs" or "relive the full show," keep complete performances and remove only accidental capture, long pre-roll, unusable sections, and repeated or badly framed duplicates. Use title cards, chapter cards, gentle crossfades, J-cuts/L-cuts, ambience bridges, and occasional B-roll covers to make the edit feel intentional without interrupting the performance.

When the user asks to remove a specific time range, apply that trim first, then rebuild downstream timeline timings and chapter markers. Call out any affected title, caption, or transition timing so the new export and upload text stay consistent.

## Retention Pass

Watch as a first-time viewer and flag any stretch where the promise, destination, question, or emotional reward becomes unclear. Repair with the smallest useful change: stronger setup, a tighter trim, B-roll proof, a short title, a breath, or a reordered beat. Effects are the last resort, not the story.

For "world-class" or cinematic feedback, perform a polish diagnosis before re-rendering: identify whether the issue is story length, transition language, titles, audio, color, camera movement, reframing, intro/outro, or lack of packaging. Add only the polish that improves the viewing experience; avoid making the video feel like a template.
