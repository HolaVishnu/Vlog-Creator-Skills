# Post-Production

Read this when audio, captions, titles, lower thirds, intro/outro, stabilization, crop/reframe, color, music, SFX, or visual polish matter.

## Audio

Start with dialogue. Choose the clearest microphone or track, reduce obvious clicks/hum/noise gently, balance voice levels, then add ambience, music, and SFX. Preserve intentional silence and room tone when they make a reaction or location feel real.

Use music and third-party SFX only when they are user-supplied, clearly licensed, or explicitly authorized. Keep music under speech and fade around important words. When rights are unknown, suggest mood, tempo, edit points, and search terms instead of selecting a track as if it is cleared.

## Stabilize, Crop, And Reframe

Stabilize only when shake distracts from the story and the crop will not damage faces, hands, products, signs, or key action. For vertical edits, reframe around the active subject and check platform safe zones before placing captions or labels. Use static crops for clarity and subtle push-ins for pacing; avoid constant automatic motion on calm material.

Check orientation metadata and visible framing separately. Some phone clips carry rotation tags that players and renderers interpret differently. If a clip appears sideways or upside down in the rendered timeline, correct it non-destructively in the derived segment or project settings, then verify the affected timeline range visually before final delivery. Preserve the original media and document the source clip ID, source range, and fix used.

## Color

Correct before grading:

1. Match exposure.
2. Set natural white balance.
3. Preserve believable skin tones.
4. Match adjacent shots.
5. Add a restrained creative grade if it supports the preset.

Document shot-specific fixes and one global look. If direct color tools are unavailable, provide editor-neutral targets and editor-specific steps in the fallback package.

## Captions, Titles, And Graphics

Captions should be accurate, readable, well timed, and clear of faces and likely platform controls. Use titles and lower thirds for names, locations, steps, dates, prices, routes, comparisons, or context that the footage does not explain. Use high contrast and short text.

Intro/outro is optional. Use a short cold open or title sting only when it increases clarity or brand feel. Keep outros useful: final payoff, next video tease, credits for licensed assets, or a clean end card. Do not add a long branded intro before the hook unless the user explicitly wants that style.

Title cards should match the edit's tone. For cinematic or concert edits, prefer clean high-contrast typography, short phrases, and subtle motion/fades over busy templates. For YouTube long-form, include an opening title, optional chapter/section cards, and a simple closing card when the footage supports it.

## Effects

Use effects to clarify, repair, or emphasize a meaningful beat. Avoid decorative clutter, rapid flashes, or graphics that compete with the creator, caption, or subject.

Transitions should feel musical or story-driven. Use crossfades, dip-to-black, match cuts, sound bridges, or motion-based cuts for scene changes; use hard cuts when preserving live performance energy matters. Avoid placing heavy transitions inside songs unless the transition hides a real camera stop or improves continuity.
